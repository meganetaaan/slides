03  When a companion's service ends
当陪伴机器人的服务停止时
Target 0:50–1:50

A communication robot can become a companion. If its proprietary service ends, that companion can die, even while its body is still beside you. When its design is closed too, the owner may have no practical way to keep it working. I wanted owners and communities to be able to repair their robots, replace services, and keep them running. Open source gives people more ways to continue, although it cannot promise that a robot will live forever. Sharing designs was one part of that idea. People who could build on them and help one another would matter too. First, I had to learn how to make a robot myself.

04  Learning to build a robot
学习亲手制作机器人
Target 1:50–2:50

Around 2018, I began sharing face-animation software for M5Stack. I liked making this small face feel alive, and I wanted to give it a moving body. After changing jobs to join a robotics startup, I learned more about mechanical design, circuits, and embedded software. I tried what I was learning in my spare time and gradually built my own robot. JavaScript was familiar to me, so the Moddable SDK helped me approach embedded development through something I already knew. This was a personal project that grew while I was learning.

05  An existing maker community
从已有的创客社区出发
Target 2:50–3:40

I also had an existing maker community around me. M5Stack offered a compact module with a screen, and makers in Japan were already sharing projects and advice. Distributors such as Switch Science helped make the hardware available. I could use the module and learn from other people's work instead of doing everything myself. Later, people with the skills to assemble a robot became some of Stack-chan's first builders. The first version was still rough, and building it took effort. Being open didn't automatically make it easy. Having people willing to try it and help was important.

07  A paper-tube octopus
一只纸筒章鱼
Target 4:30–5:20

That first version was simple, and I wanted people to feel comfortable showing a simple creation too. There is a childhood memory behind that feeling. In kindergarten, I made an octopus from a paper tube. I was told it wasn't complicated enough. So I made two octopuses. That still wasn't enough. I sometimes remember this when I think about what makes something worth showing. With Stack-chan, I want people to be able to enjoy what they made and proudly call it a robot. It can be a beginning, even before they know what to add next.

08  Two motors and a simple face
两个电机，一张简单的脸
Target 5:20–6:20

That is why I want to keep the basic Stack-chan approachable: two motors and a simple face. A small movement can already make the face feel alive. People can learn through building and changing it, with help where they need it. More complicated versions are welcome too. Someone might add arms or wheels, or try a different mechanism. I want those experiments to continue while the small two-motor version remains available as a starting point. The aim is to leave room for a first attempt and for the ideas that come afterward.

09  A case for a different motor
为不同的电机改造外壳
Target 6:20–7:10

One early contribution showed what that room could mean. A maker known as tomorrow56 had a different motor available, so they modified the case to fit an SG-90 servo. They could use the parts they had and share the result for somebody else to build. It was a specific mechanical change, and also another possible starting point. Participation could begin with one person's circumstances: a part they already owned and a change they wanted to make. Their version gave the next person another option.

10  Other boards are welcome
欢迎使用其他开发板
Target 7:10–8:00

I welcomed versions using other boards from an early stage too. People used Arduino, obniz, and other ARM-based boards. M5Stack had given my robot a good starting point, but I didn't want my hardware choice to decide who could join. People had tools they liked and knowledge they wanted to use. Bringing those things made sense. The photograph shows the variety people brought to a maker event. These versions came from their experiments; I didn't have a plan for which platform should come next. Then I began noticing participation that went beyond building robot hardware.

11  Many kinds of making
各种各样的创作方式
Target 8:00–9:00

This is Stack-san, a wearable creation by Latte. Its scale and the way it meets people are very different from my desk robot. Other people drew comics or made outfits for their Stack-chans. Engineering remained important, and now people were finding more ways to take part alongside it.

12  A familiar face, your own expression
熟悉的脸，不同的表达
Target 9:00–10:00

Looking at those creations gave me a hypothesis. Stack-chan's simple face leaves room for people to add their own expression through clothes, stories, or behavior. At the same time, it remains recognizable as Stack-chan. Perhaps that helps someone make something personal while feeling connected to other makers. I haven't proved that this causes the community to grow. It is a way I currently understand what I have seen. I didn't design a successful growth plan in advance. I noticed people doing unexpected things and began asking what would help them continue.

13  Takao's kits
让更多人动手组装
Target 10:00–10:50

Some of that support came from other community members. Takao prepared and distributed kits and organized gatherings where people could work on their robots together. Preparing kits reliably and helping people through assembly require different skills and sustained effort. A kit reduced an assembly difficulty; a gathering gave people somewhere to ask for help. Takao could provide support that I couldn't have offered alone. This is one way to help people get started. Another concerns what people can call and share once they have made something.

14  A shared name for our creations
让大家都能使用这个名字
Target 10:50–11:55

The history of the name is a separate story, going back to kit development at my former employer. Someone suggested a trademark defensively, so another party couldn't take exclusive control of the name. I wanted individuals and companies to be able to use Stack-chan as a shared character and name. The trademark came first; later, I wrote guidelines for derivative works. My aim was to support free use under published guidelines and reduce uncertainty about creating something. This is an intention, not blanket permission for every use. Code licenses, the name, and other people's photographs still have different rules.

15  A place to ask and share
有地方提问，也有地方分享
Target 11:55–12:40

Clearer rules answer one kind of question. People also need somewhere to ask practical questions and find one another. Discord gives them a place to show work in progress and talk about what they are trying. Someone might be choosing a motor, changing an expression, or making an outfit. A conversation can reveal that another person has tried something similar. I have spent time supporting that space as the project has grown. A person's first contribution can be a question, and answering it can help somebody else continue.

16  A role you can name yourself
自己定义在社区中的角色
Target 12:40–13:35

I also made a business-card editor that anyone can use. The important part for me is that people can name their own way of taking part. They can introduce the role they want to have in the Stack-chan community, without waiting for me to assign it. The card makes that self-introduction visible when people meet. It can begin a conversation about what someone likes making and how they want to be involved. I want participation to include that freedom to say who you are in relation to the project, alongside the freedom to change its hardware or software.

17  A robot's birthday party
机器人的生日会
Target 13:35–14:40

At a birthday party, those different forms of participation meet. People bring robots, outfits, and stories about what they have tried. A technical achievement and a first attempt can both be worth sharing. These gatherings also let people meet someone they may later ask for help, or help themselves. I discover work that happened without my involvement. That matters for the original question of how a robot can keep going. A party doesn't guarantee long-term maintenance, but the knowledge and relationships people build can give them more options. I hope those connections continue in many places, even as individual people's circumstances change.

18  AI Stack-chan
社区带来了 AI 对话
Target 14:40–15:40

Another opening came through AI. A community member, robo8080, connected Stack-chan to an LLM and created AI Stack-chan. Other people shared tutorials and further integrations. I had been interested in spoken dialogue since my student days, but hadn't expected it to become such a large part of Stack-chan's appeal. The technology improved, somebody wanted to try it, and there was already a robot to build on. This contribution gave people interested in AI a way to experiment with a physical robot. More recently, a ready-made product has made that starting point available in another form.

19  M5StackChan brings new people
M5StackChan 带来新的参与者
Target 15:40–16:40

M5Stack introduced the commercial product M5StackChan. I had very little involvement in developing it. I welcome it as another way to begin, especially for people whose starting interest is AI and who want to try it in something physical. They can start with the interaction they want to explore, then choose what to change. They may meet people interested in hardware, outfits, or other forms of making. I am interested in what happens when those interests meet. The community's AI experiments and this product are different contributions, and both create possibilities that I didn't have to design myself.

20  My next experiment
我想尝试的下一步
Target 16:40–17:35

These possibilities also change what I want to try. One idea is several Stack-chans talking together, with a person listening and joining when something interests them. Which robot speaks next? Where does it look? How does it notice that someone wants to join? Before LLM integration, I spent a lot of time on blinking, gaze, and small movements. A turn or a pause can shape an interaction even with two motors. That interest is still with me. This is a direction to explore, rather than a finished system I am showing today.

21  10,000 people
连接世界各地的一万人
Target 17:35–18:25

Alongside my own experiments, I have a longer-term goal: a community of ten thousand people around the world. It is a future goal, not our current size, and I set it after the project was underway. Working toward it means making room for more kinds of participation and helping people continue. I can't predict what everyone will want to make. What I can do is keep learning from what people are already doing, and help with the difficulties that get in their way. That is the direction I want to take as the community grows.
