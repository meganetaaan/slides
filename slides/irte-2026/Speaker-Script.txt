SECTION 1  A Personal Project

03  When a companion's service ends
当陪伴机器人的服务停止时
Target 1:00–2:10

A communication robot can become a companion. If its proprietary service ends, that companion can die, even while its body is still beside you. When its design is closed too, the owner may have no practical way to keep it working. I wanted owners and communities to be able to repair their robots, replace services, and keep them running. Open source gives people more ways to continue, although it cannot promise that a robot will live forever. This story begins with my own making. The circles will grow as different ways of taking part overlap. They are not a population chart or a ladder of maturity. Personal making stays at the center. First, I had to learn how to build a robot myself.

04  Learning to build a robot
学习亲手制作机器人
Target 2:10–2:59

Around 2018, I began sharing face-animation software for M5Stack. I liked making this small face feel alive, and I wanted to give it a moving body. After changing jobs to join a robotics startup, I learned more about mechanical design, circuits, and embedded software. I tried what I was learning in my spare time and gradually built my own robot. JavaScript was familiar to me, so the Moddable SDK helped me approach embedded development through something I already knew. This was a personal project that grew while I was learning.

05  The birth of Stack-chan
Stack-chan 的诞生
Target 2:59–3:47

Here is the tweet where I shared Stack-chan in my hand. The battery was in, so it could move without a power cable. In the post, I wrote that it had become a real robot I could hold on my palm. It was still a rough first version, but the small face now had a moving body. I was learning as I made it in my spare time. This is the moment I wanted to show you, before I knew what other people would go on to make.

06  A paper-tube octopus
一只纸筒章鱼
Target 3:47–4:39

That first version was simple, and I wanted people to feel comfortable showing a simple creation too. There is a childhood memory behind that feeling. In kindergarten, I made an octopus from a paper tube. I was told it wasn't complicated enough. So I made two octopuses. That still wasn't enough. I sometimes remember this when I think about what makes something worth showing. With Stack-chan, I want people to be able to enjoy what they made and proudly call it a robot. It can be a beginning, even before they know what to add next.

07  Two motors and a simple face
两个电机，一张简单的脸
Target 4:39–5:34

That is why I want to keep the basic Stack-chan approachable: two motors and a simple face. A small movement can already make the face feel alive. People can learn through building and changing it, with help where they need it. More complicated versions are welcome too. Someone might add arms or wheels, or try a different mechanism. I want those experiments to continue while the small two-motor version remains available as a starting point. The aim is to leave room for a first attempt and for the ideas that come afterward. Then other makers began adapting it to their own needs.

SECTION 2  Other Makers

08  An existing maker community
从已有的创客社区出发
Target 5:34–6:35

The next circle adds other makers, while personal projects remain inside it. I already had a maker community around me. M5Stack offered a compact module with a screen, and makers in Japan were sharing projects and advice. Distributors such as Switch Science helped make the hardware available. I could use the module and learn from other people's work instead of doing everything myself. People with the skills to assemble a robot became some of Stack-chan's first builders. The first version was still rough, and building it took effort. Being open didn't automatically make it easy. Having people willing to try it and help was important.

09  A case for a different motor
为不同的电机改造外壳
Target 6:35–7:21

One early contribution showed what that room could mean. A maker known as tomorrow56 had a different motor available, so they modified the case to fit an SG-90 servo. Participation could begin with one person's circumstances: a part they already owned and a change they wanted to make. Their version gave the next person another option.

11  Keeping the shared name available
让共同的名字持续可用
Target 8:14–9:04

The history of the name goes back to kit development at my former employer. Someone suggested a trademark defensively, so another party couldn't take exclusive control of the name. I wanted individuals and companies to be able to use Stack-chan as a shared character and name. The trademark came before the derivative-work guidelines that I will discuss later. It was one practical way of keeping a shared name available while different people worked on kits and other versions. The name and the code licenses are different parts of supporting independent makers.

让更多人动手组装
Target 9:04–9:56

Another kind of support came from Takao. He prepared and distributed kits and organized gatherings where people could work on their robots together. 各种各样的创作方式

This circle adds more ways to create; the earlier ways are still here. This is Stack-san, a wearable creation by Latte. Its scale and the way it meets people are very different from my desk robot. 14  A familiar face, your own expression
熟悉的脸，不同的表达
Target 10:56–11:46

Looking at those creations gave me a hypothesis. Stack-chan's simple face leaves room for people to add their own expression through clothes, stories, or behavior. At the same time, it remains recognizable as Stack-chan. Perhaps that helps someone make something personal while feeling connected to other makers. I haven't proved that this causes the community to grow. It is a way I currently understand what I have seen. I didn't design a successful growth plan in advance. I noticed people doing unexpected things and began asking what would help them continue.

15  Guidelines and a place to ask
创作指南与交流的空间
Target 11:46–12:48

After the trademark came the derivative-work guidelines. My aim was to support free use under published guidelines and reduce uncertainty about creating something. That intention is not blanket permission for every use: code licenses, the name, and other people's photographs still have different rules. People also need somewhere to ask practical questions. Discord gives them a place to show work in progress and talk about a motor, an expression, or an outfit. A conversation can reveal that someone has tried something similar. I have spent time supporting that space as the project has grown. Guidelines and conversations support these overlapping activities. A first contribution can be a question, and answering it can help somebody else continue.

16  A role you can name yourself
自己定义在社区中的角色
Target 12:48–13:41

I also made a business-card editor that anyone can use. The important part for me is that people can name their own way of taking part. They can introduce the role they want to have in the Stack-chan community, without waiting for me to assign it. The card makes that self-introduction visible when people meet. It can begin a conversation about what someone likes making and how they want to be involved. I want participation to include that freedom to say who you are in relation to the project, alongside the freedom to change its hardware or software.

SECTION 4  New Ways In

18  AI Stack-chan
社区带来了 AI 对话
Target 14:33–15:29

This circle adds new entrances; it doesn't replace the earlier ones. A community member, robo8080, connected Stack-chan to an LLM and created AI Stack-chan. Other people shared tutorials and further integrations. I had been interested in spoken dialogue since my student days, but hadn't expected it to become such a large part of Stack-chan's appeal. The technology improved, somebody wanted to try it, and there was already a robot to build on. This contribution gave people interested in AI a way to experiment with a physical robot. A ready-made product offers a separate way to begin.

SECTION 5  What Comes Next

20  My next experiment
我想尝试的下一步
Target 16:23–17:25

The dotted outside circle is what comes next, rather than a completed stage. The earlier circles, including my own making at the center, stay with it. One idea I want to try is several Stack-chans talking together, with a person listening and joining when something interests them. Which robot speaks next? Where does it look? How does it notice that someone wants to join? Before LLM integration, I spent a lot of time on blinking, gaze, and small movements. A turn or a pause can shape an interaction even with two motors. This is a direction to explore, rather than a finished system I am showing today.

21  10,000 people
连接世界各地的一万人
Target 17:25–18:19

Alongside my own experiments, I have a longer-term goal: a community of ten thousand people around the world. It is a future goal, not our current size. The circles are not a scale for that number; they show overlapping ways of taking part. Working toward the goal means making room for more kinds of participation and helping people continue. I can't predict what everyone will want to make. What I can do is keep learning from what people are already doing, and help with the difficulties that get in their way. I want to keep making things myself as well.
